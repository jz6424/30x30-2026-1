How LED light therapy works

LED stands for light-emitting diode. A beauty LED device shines low-energy light of a specific color, or wavelength, onto your skin. Unlike lasers or IPL, it doesn't heat or injure tissue, so there's no recovery time.

Scientists call the effect photobiomodulation. Cells absorb certain wavelengths, which is thought to boost energy production in the mitochondria. In skin, that may support the cells (fibroblasts) that make collagen and elastin and help calm inflammation.

Wavelength is measured in nanometers (nm). The rule of thumb: the longer the wavelength, the deeper the light reaches. Blue stays near the surface, red reaches the dermis, and near-infrared goes deeper still.

What each color does

Red light, about 630 to 660 nm

The most studied color for skin. Red light reaches the dermis, where collagen is made, and is used for fine lines, texture and firmness. It's the main light in the Halo mask (269 red LEDs) and in the Moonlight masks (630 nm).

Orange and amber light, about 590 to 620 nm

Between red and yellow. Used for brightness, a more even-looking tone and calming redness. Halo includes 269 orange LEDs that run alongside red and blue.

Blue light, about 415 nm

Stays in the upper layers of skin, where the bacteria associated with acne (C. acnes) live. These bacteria contain light-sensitive molecules called porphyrins, and blue light is used for mild to moderate breakouts. Halo includes 269 blue LEDs; Moonlight masks use 415 nm.

Green light, about 520 nm

Used for uneven tone and the look of sun spots. Available on Moonlight and Moonlight Pro. The evidence is thinner than for red and blue.

Yellow, cyan, purple and white

Moonlight Pro offers seven colors. Yellow is used for brightening, cyan (about 490 nm) for calming redness, purple (about 390 nm) pairs red and blue effects, and white mixes wavelengths. Treat these as gentle complements to red and blue, which have the most research.

Near-infrared, about 800 to 850 nm

Invisible to the eye and reaches deepest. Often paired with red for anti-ageing. The Avorla Golden Glow wand combines red LEDs with four infrared LEDs and a warming head.

What the evidence says

Being honest about the science helps you set the right expectations:

  • Red and near-infrared: several small controlled studies report improvements in fine lines, skin roughness and collagen density after 8 to 12 weeks of regular sessions.
  • Blue: studies show reductions in mild to moderate inflammatory acne, often better when combined with red.
  • Other colors: green, yellow and the rest have less research behind them.
  • Consistency matters most. Benefits fade if you stop, which is why a comfortable, hands-free routine you'll actually keep is worth more than peak power.

LED is a cosmetic, low-risk routine. It won't replace retinoids, sunscreen or in-office procedures, but it fits alongside them.

Beyond LED: the other technologies in our devices

Microcurrent and EMS

Very low electrical currents that make facial muscles contract and relax, used for a lifted, toned look and better circulation. Effects are temporary and build with regular use.

Radio frequency (RF) and high frequency

Gently heats the deeper layers of the skin to encourage collagen remodelling and a firmer look. SuperNova uses RF heating with 25 mm and 45 mm probes and a conductive gel; Zenith pairs high-frequency warmth with red and blue LED and micro-vibration. Keep RF devices moving; never hold them in one spot.

Vibration and massage

Boosts circulation, supports lymphatic drainage for less puffiness, and helps serums spread and absorb.

Low-level laser therapy (LLLT) for hair

Low-power red laser or LED light applied to the scalp to support hair growth in pattern hair loss. See the hair regrowth range.

IPL hair removal

Intense pulsed light targets the pigment in hair to reduce regrowth over repeated sessions. Unlike LED, IPL works through heat and isn't suitable for every skin tone, so follow the device's skin tone chart. See the hair removal range.

Safety

  • LED devices are UV-free and don't tan or burn skin.
  • Close your eyes and use the eye cover. Don't look directly at the LEDs.
  • Ask a doctor first if you're pregnant, have epilepsy or another light-sensitive condition, have an eye condition, or take photosensitizing medication such as isotretinoin.
  • Skip sessions on broken skin, active infections or right after in-office treatments.
  • Stop if you notice lasting redness or irritation.
