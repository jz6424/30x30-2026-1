LED face masks, answered

The questions people ask most before buying, with honest answers. For the science, see the technology guide.

Results

Do LED face masks actually work?

Small clinical studies of red and near-infrared light have found modest improvements in fine lines, skin texture and collagen density, and blue light has evidence for mild to moderate acne. Results build gradually with consistent use over weeks. LED is a gentle, no-downtime routine, not a replacement for in-office procedures.

How long until I see results from an LED mask?

Most people use LED 3 to 5 times a week. Changes in glow and redness can show within a few weeks; fine lines and texture usually take 8 to 12 weeks of regular sessions. Take photos in the same light every two weeks so you can see the change.

Which LED color is best for wrinkles?

Red light, around 630 to 660 nm, has the most research for fine lines and texture, often paired with near-infrared. Halo includes 269 red LEDs, and the Moonlight masks use 630 nm red.

Which LED color is best for acne?

Blue light, around 415 nm, targets the bacteria associated with breakouts. It is often combined with red to help calm redness. Halo runs blue and red together; Moonlight and Moonlight Pro let you select blue on its own.

Do results last if I stop?

Benefits fade gradually if you stop completely. After the first two to three months, many people drop to one or two sessions a week to maintain results.

Safety

Does an LED face mask hurt or get hot?

No. LEDs are not lasers and do not burn or peel the skin. You may feel slight warmth. The Halo mask is 100% UV-free, so it does not tan or cause sun damage.

Is the Halo mask safe for my eyes?

Halo has a patented blackout eye cover so light doesn't shine into your eyes. Keep your eyes closed during the cycle. If you have an eye condition, a light-sensitivity condition such as epilepsy, or take a photosensitizing medication such as isotretinoin, ask your doctor before using any LED device.

Can I use LED light therapy on darker skin tones?

LED light therapy is generally considered suitable for all skin tones because, unlike IPL hair removal or some lasers, it does not work by heating pigment. If you have melasma or a history of hyperpigmentation, start with shorter sessions and talk to a dermatologist.

Who should not use an LED mask?

Ask a doctor first if you are pregnant, have epilepsy or another light-sensitive condition, have an eye condition, have skin cancer or a suspicious lesion, or take photosensitizing medication. Skip sessions on broken skin or right after in-office treatments.

Is LED the same as a laser or IPL?

No. Lasers and IPL use concentrated light that heats tissue or pigment. LED uses low-energy light that does not heat or injure skin, which is why it has no downtime and suits daily routines.

Using your device

How often should I use the Halo mask?

Use one 25-minute cycle 3 to 5 times a week to start. Once you are happy with your skin, one or two sessions a week can help maintain results.

How do I turn on the Halo mask?

Press the touch pad on the upper right of the mask to power on, then press it again to start the 25-minute cycle. To cover your neck, line up the magnetic neck cover with the bottom of the mask.

Should I apply skincare before or after LED?

Use the mask on clean, dry skin with no makeup, sunscreen or heavy cream, so nothing blocks the light. Apply serums and moisturizer afterwards.

What is the difference between Halo and Moonlight Pro?

Halo runs red, orange and blue light together in one 25-minute cycle with 807 LEDs and a neck cover, so there are no settings to choose. Moonlight Pro has 150 LED chips and seven selectable colors with adjustable brightness and time, for people who want to target one concern per session.

Can men use LED face masks?

Yes. LED works the same way on all skin. Shave beforehand if you want light to reach the skin evenly along the jaw.

How do I clean my mask?

Unplug or switch off the mask, then wipe it with a soft damp cloth. Don't submerge it in water.
